THE ROADS NOT TAKEN

Nine ideas from computing's golden age, rebuilt. Because they were right.

Vannevar Bush — The Memex
1945 · "As We May Think" · Atlantic Monthly
A personal device for storing all your books, records, and communications, navigable by associative trails — personal paths through knowledge that mirror how the mind actually works.
"Wholly new forms of encyclopedias will appear, ready-made with a trail of associative trails running through them."
What we got instead: hierarchical file systems and keyword search. The trail — the personal, shareable path through knowledge — was lost.
memex/
Newell, Shaw & Simon — IPL
1956 · RAND Corporation · The Logic Theorist
The first list-processing language, built to power the first AI program. The Logic Theorist proved 38 of 52 theorems from Principia Mathematica. Introduced linked lists, recursive subroutines, and symbolic computation.
"We invented a computer program capable of thinking non-numerically, and thereby solved the venerable mind-body problem." — Herbert Simon (somewhat cheekily)
IPL influenced LISP, which survived — but mostly as the language you configure Emacs in. The dream of machines that reason symbolically was buried by the AI Winter.
ipl/
J.C.R. Licklider — Man-Computer Symbiosis
1960 · "Man-Computer Symbiosis" · IRE Transactions
Not artificial intelligence. Symbiosis. Humans bring intuition and judgment; computers bring speed and tirelessness. Together, they think better than either alone.
"The hope is that, in not too many years, human brains and computing machines will be coupled together very tightly, and that the resulting partnership will think as no human brain has ever thought."
Licklider funded the ARPANET. But his vision of networked collaborative thinking became... social media. The "Intergalactic Computer Network" became the attention economy.
symbiosis/
Ted Nelson — Xanadu
1960 · "Computer Lib / Dream Machines" (1974)
Universal hypertext with bidirectional links (if I link to you, you know), transclusion (quoting by reference, not copying), and permanent versioning. Nothing is ever deleted.
"A file is not bumped. You just add on. And you never throw anything away."
Tim Berners-Lee built the web with one-way links and no transclusion. URLs rot. Sources are obscured. Nelson called the web "a grotesque parody" of his vision. He's not wrong.
xanadu/
Doug Engelbart — NLS / Augmentation
1962 · "Augmenting Human Intellect" · SRI
The computer as a tool for augmenting human intellect. Multiple simultaneous views of the same structure. Real-time collaboration. The 1968 "Mother of All Demos" showed the future — then the future went elsewhere.
"By 'augmenting human intellect' we mean increasing the capability of a man to approach a complex problem situation, to gain comprehension to suit his particular needs, and to derive solutions to problems."
The mouse survived. The five-finger chord keyboard didn't. Multi-view structured documents became PowerPoint. Collaborative augmentation became Google Docs.
nls/
Ivan Sutherland — Sketchpad
1963 · MIT PhD Thesis · TX-2 Computer
The first graphical interface. But Sketchpad wasn't about drawing — it was about constraints. You declare that two lines are parallel, and the system maintains that relationship no matter what you change.
"Sketchpad made it possible for a man and a computer to converse rapidly through the medium of line drawings."
Constraints became CAD software — powerful but specialized. Consumer interfaces regressed to direct manipulation of pixels. The idea that a computer should understand the RELATIONSHIPS between things, not just their positions, was largely forgotten.
sketchpad/
Seymour Papert — LOGO
1967 · MIT AI Lab · "Mindstorms" (1980)
Mathematics as a physical, embodied experience. The turtle is "an object to think with." Children don't learn math by being taught — they learn by building things and debugging them.
"In many schools today, the weights of teaching are so heavily on the side of telling that the child's natural capacity for learning through exploration and construction is stunted."
Schools got computers and used them for "computer literacy": typing tests and spreadsheets. LOGO was marginalized. The idea that programming is a medium for mathematical thought was replaced by "learn to code" as vocational training.
logo/
Carl Hewitt — PLANNER
1969 · MIT AI Lab · Goal-Directed Reasoning
Instead of forward-chaining through rules, state what you want to be true and let the system figure out how. Backward chaining: work from the goal, not from the start.
PLANNER introduced pattern-directed invocation: procedures are called not by name, but by the PATTERN of the goal they can satisfy.
PLANNER influenced Prolog. SHRDLU briefly convinced everyone that natural language understanding was solved. Then the AI Winter hit and symbolic reasoning was abandoned for decades.
planner/
Alan Kay — The Dynabook
1972 · Xerox PARC · "A Personal Computer for Children of All Ages"
A portable, reactive computing medium where everything is a live object that can be inspected, modified, and composed. The boundary between using and programming dissolves. Not a consumption device — a creation medium.
"The best way to predict the future is to invent it."
The iPad looks like a Dynabook but isn't one. It's a consumption device with an App Store. You can't inspect how any app works. You can't modify anything. You can't compose apps together. Kay's vision of a medium became a marketplace.
dynabook/
